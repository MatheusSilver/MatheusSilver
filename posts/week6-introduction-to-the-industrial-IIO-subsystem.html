Introduction to the Industrial I/O (IIO) Subsystem

Objective

This week had a slightly simpler practical task, but a heavy theoretical part. Our objective this week was to properly understand how the Linux IIO subsystem works, or at least the fundamentals behind it. For that, we followed two tutorials: the first one explained the structure, and the second one explained and guided us on how we could modify the example driver.

Challenges

Regarding the theoretical part, I did not face many issues, since Rodrigo's kind of "tricky" approach was something I was already used to. My first programming experience was trying to understand Friday Night Funkin' code, which was honestly much messier than Linux Kernel code.

Because of that, seeing Rodrigo's suggestions felt like a kind of validation of how I used to approach code reading.

Experience

After exploring the theoretical part, I went through the experimental part of the tutorial. The objective in this part was to add a new sensor for a compass, and to do that we needed to modify a struct and add new indexes into an enum.

A small side note here: it was actually a surprise to me that Linux Kernel uses enums, since I used to think enums were something specific to Java, and once in a while I hear people making some kind of "war" between languages.

Side note apart, after those changes we finally defined new channels using iio_chan_spec. After that, we proceeded with the initialization and some small adjustments, and the attributes appeared easily.

To be perfectly honest, the most complex task was understanding the code itself. However, I do believe it became much easier to follow the tutorial after understanding what we were doing.
